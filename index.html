The launching point for India’s compliance intelligence.

In April 2026, India replaced its 65-year-old Income-tax Act: 819 sections became 536, every section number changed, and every AI assistant in the world started citing repealed law with total confidence. Prasthana exists to fix that class of problem — machine-verified law, not model guesswork. Our first product family, Karsastra, drafts tax-notice replies where every statutory citation is checked against a verified engine before a professional ever sees it.

01 · findings

No frontier model scores above 58.5% on the new Act.

We built the itact-785 benchmark: 785 tax-law tasks from the Income-tax Act 2025, locked and hash-verified before any model saw a single question, graded by strict entity match with no LLM judge. Then we scored every model our subscriptions could reach. The general-purpose models came nowhere near usable — and the task families that matter most for professional work scored zero on every one of them. Only our own 4B fine-tune, trained to work with the engine, crosses the usable line.

observed failure · deepseek-v4.1-flash · October 2026 stderr.log
ask deepseek-v4.1-flash --act 2025 --challan 2026-07 --date-bound
Q: “Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 — for a challan due in July 2026 — what payment code applies to interest on securities?”
A: “1001 Interest on securities · 1002 Dividends · 1003 Interest on Government securities…”
the model confidently recited the repealed 1961-Act ITNS-281 code table for a 2026 question.
The engine’s verified code table answers the same 107 tasks by lookup, machine-checked: 100%.

03 · detail

Five findings, machine-graded

[F1] code scheme Models can’t even emit a 2025-Act payment code

107 tasks asking for TDS payment codes under the new scheme. General-purpose models refused, invented codes (“payment code: 94”), or pattern-matched to whichever 10xx-looking table they memorized — including the repealed 1961-Act ITNS table, as above.

glm-5.3-flash: 0 / 107 · deepseek: 4 / 107 · gpt-oss:120b: 0 / 107 · glm-5.3, qwen3.5 base: 0 / 107 · karsastra-4b-v44: 97% (104/107) (engine answers the tier at 100%)
[F2] section mapping Models cite the old Act for 2026 questions

“A notice cites old s.139(5) — what is its replacement under the 2025 Act?” Gold: s.263(5). One model answered “section 134” — an invention. Every CA in India is living this exact judgment right now; the engine’s 3,792-pair transition map answers it deterministically.

glm-5.3-flash: 32% · deepseek-v4.1-flash: 0% · gpt-oss:120b: 0% · engine map: 3,792 pairs, deterministic
[F3] 4-part compliance Zero passes on the task a reply actually needs

Given a payment, produce code + section + rate + deposit date — all four correct. 99 ICAI-style tasks. No general-purpose model (glm, deepseek, qwen base, gpt-oss) completed a single one. Our current fine-tune scores 96/99 — the tier a notice reply actually needs is now model-solved, with every emitted entity engine-checked.

glm-5.3 · deepseek · gpt-oss: 0 / 99 · karsastra-4b (v4): 18 / 99 · karsastra-4b-v44: 96 / 99
[F4] the boundary The confusion window is measurable

Transitional fact: a payment credited 1 June 2026 is governed by the new Act, not the one being repealed. The strongest general-purpose model misassigns the governing Act on roughly one-third of boundary cases — on the precise judgment the whole product depends on. Our v44 fine-tune: 11/11.

glm-5.3: 6/9 · glm-5.3-flash: 5/8 · deepseek & gpt-oss: 3/9
[F5] what survives Pretraining still knows general knowledge — so let it keep prose, surrender facts

Rates (78%), TDS amounts (78%), dates (62%), act selection (62%) — the general-knowledge tier survives. This defines the product split: the engine owns the facts (655 code-family entries, the 3,792-pair transition map, compliance tables — deterministically), the model owns prose assembly around them, and every citation emitted is machine-checked against the engine before the reply ships. 100% on the citations, or the system refuses to emit one.

surviving tier · rates: 78% · TDS amounts: 78% · dates: 62% · act selection: 62%
04 · our model

A 4-billion-parameter model out-scores every frontier model we tested.

karsastra-4b is a fine-tuned Qwen 3.5 4B trained not to memorize the Act but to work with the engine: resolve a lookup the right way, cite the right table, and hold the prose together. On the locked set it scores 89.4% — far beyond the best frontier result (58.5%) — at 100% on the rate-tier and higher-rate no-PAN rulings, and near-total on the payment-code and 4-part compliance tiers every general-purpose model zeroed.

05 · approach

The model doesn’t own the law. The engine does.

The five findings converge on one architecture. It is what makes a correct answer provable rather than probable.

Deterministic engine

  • 655 code-family entries, Gazette-sourced
  • 3,792-pair old↔new transition map
  • Compliance tables: code + section + rate + dates
  • Act-boundary rules (CBDT FAQ-verified)
  • Machine-verified provenance on every row
──facts──▶ deterministic
lookup

Verify

  • every citation re-checked post-generation
  • refuses rather than emit an unverified cite
  • 100% on the citations, or no reply
◀──cites── machine-checked

Language model

  • reads the notice + client documents
  • selects the applicable engine facts
  • assembles the reply prose around them

Why a locked eval, graded this strictly

  1. Locked before scoring — 785 tasks, sha256 14ffaa7d…, frozen before any model was run. Models can’t study for it.
  2. Strict entity match, no LLM judge — a pass requires all gold entities. The grader was proven two ways: gold answers all parse (785/785), and mutated golds fail (it discriminates).
  3. Independently replicated — a separate inspect-ai pipeline with a translated grader scored 0 verdict differences on shared records.
  4. Regenerable weekly — the confusion window is live; the leaderboard gets new legs (including external models PR’d with their own keys) and re-publishes.

What we are building on it

Karsastra-Tax — for CA firms handling GST and income-tax notices: upload the notice and the client’s papers, get a structured first-draft reply citing the correct Act — old or new — with every section, code, and form number machine-verified. A 3–6 hour reply becomes a 30-minute review.

Beyond tax, the same engine-plus-verifier pattern is the lab’s thesis for Indian compliance domains where the law is precise, high-stakes, and recently rewritten — the places general chat models are the most confidently wrong.

06 · about the lab

The name

Prasthana (Sanskrit: प्रस्थान) — “the setting forth, the launching point.” In Vedanta the prasthana-trayi are the three canonical foundational texts from which all reasoning sets out. We liked a name that is literally about correct foundations: begin from the verified source, then build everything on top of it.

The lab, plainly

Prasthana is a small, independent applied AI lab. We run our own agent fleet and training pipelines daily, and our engineering discipline comes from surviving them: spend containment, locked evals, deterministic verification, refusal over hallucination. The first family built here is Karsastra (tax). Later: Prasthana-Bharat, a frontier Indian-laws base model trained on the verified corpus the engine is built from.
